Practical Assessment Task • Phase 3

Online banking in South Africa

Digital banking has replaced the queue at the counter for millions of South Africans. This investigation asks what that convenience has cost us in security — and whether consumers are ready for the risk.

See the findings

Introduction to the topic

Online banking has become an essential part of everyday life, changing the way consumers move money, pay accounts and shop. A person who once had to take a taxi into town, stand in a queue and carry cash home can now do the same transaction from a phone in under a minute, at any hour of the day.

However, the growth of digital banking also comes with risks. Cyber fraud, phishing scams, SIM-swap attacks and stolen card details have grown alongside it, and criminals no longer need to be anywhere near their victim. Banks have moved much of the responsibility for staying safe onto the customer, which means the customer's own knowledge is now part of the security system.

This investigation will explore the impact of online banking on South African consumers — both the convenience it has created and the risks it has introduced.

Purpose of this investigation: to determine how online banking affects the security and the convenience of South African consumers, and to find out whether users understand the risks well enough to protect themselves.

Focus question

How does online banking affect the security and convenience of South African consumers?

Questions this investigation answers

  1. What is online banking, and which services does it include?
  2. Which benefits do South African consumers gain from banking online?
  3. Which types of fraud most commonly target online banking users?
  4. How aware are ordinary users of these risks?
  5. What can a consumer do to bank online more safely?
