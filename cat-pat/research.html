What is online banking?

Online banking (also called internet banking or digital banking) is a service that lets a customer manage a bank account over the internet instead of visiting a branch. The customer logs in with a username and password, and confirms transactions with a one-time PIN or an approval in the bank's app.

In South Africa the service is offered by every major bank, and is normally used in three ways:

  • Internet banking — through a web browser on a computer.
  • Mobile banking apps — the bank's own app on a smartphone, usually secured with a fingerprint or face scan.
  • Cellphone or USSD banking — short codes dialled on a basic phone, which need no data connection.

Typical services include checking a balance, transferring money, paying accounts, buying airtime and electricity, freezing a lost card and opening a savings account — all without a bank employee being involved.

Benefits for the consumer

  • Available at any time. The service does not close at 15:30 or on public holidays.
  • Saves travel and time. No taxi fare into town and no queue, which matters most to people far from a branch.
  • Cheaper transactions. An electronic transfer usually costs a fraction of a cash withdrawal or a branch payment.
  • Less cash carried. Paying digitally reduces the risk of being robbed on the way home.
  • Better record keeping. Every transaction is listed and searchable, which helps with budgeting.
  • Instant control. A stolen card can be frozen from the app in seconds.

Types of online banking fraud

Almost all of these attacks work by tricking the person rather than breaking the bank's computers. That is why consumer awareness matters so much.

Table 1: Common digital banking crimes in South Africa
Type of fraud How it works Warning sign
Phishing A fake e-mail or SMS that looks like it comes from the bank, linking to a copied login page that captures the password. A link asking you to "verify" or "reactivate" your account.
Vishing A phone call from someone claiming to be the bank's fraud department, who talks the victim into reading out an OTP. Any caller who asks for a one-time PIN.
SIM swap The criminal transfers the victim's cellphone number onto a new SIM card, so the bank's OTPs arrive on their phone. Your phone suddenly loses signal for no reason.
Card-not-present fraud Stolen card numbers are used to buy goods online, where no physical card and no PIN are needed. Small unfamiliar amounts appearing on a statement.
Malware and fake apps A downloaded app or attachment records what the victim types, including the banking password. A banking app installed from anywhere but the official store.
Public Wi-Fi interception Traffic on an open, unsecured network is captured by someone else connected to the same network. Banking on free Wi-Fi with no password.

The common thread: in every row above, the criminal needs the victim to hand something over — a password, an OTP or a tap on a link. No bank will ever ask a customer for a one-time PIN.

How to bank online safely

  1. Never share a one-time PIN, password or card CVV number with anybody, including someone claiming to be from the bank.
  2. Type the bank's web address in yourself instead of clicking a link in an e-mail or SMS.
  3. Download banking apps only from the official Google Play Store or Apple App Store.
  4. Switch on transaction notifications so any unexpected payment is noticed immediately.
  5. Use a long, unique password for banking, and never reuse a social media password.
  6. Avoid banking on free public Wi-Fi; use mobile data instead.
  7. Report a phone that loses signal unexpectedly — it may be a SIM swap in progress.
  8. Check statements every month, even for small amounts.

How the data was collected

Two kinds of data were used in this investigation:

  • Primary data — a questionnaire completed by learners, family members and community members, asking about their own online banking habits and their awareness of fraud. These answers were captured in a spreadsheet in Phase 2 and are reported on the Findings page.
  • Secondary data — published information from South African banking and security organisations, listed in full on the Bibliography page.

Useful organisations consulted for background information include SABRIC, the South African Banking Risk Information Centre, and the South African Reserve Bank. (These links open in a new tab.)
